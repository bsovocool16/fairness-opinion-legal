# Rule 13e-3 or conventional: why it is asked first

A going-private transaction (an affiliate, a controlling holder or management on the buy side) is a Rule 13e-3 transaction:
the issuer files a Schedule 13E-3 with the proxy, and Item 1015 of Regulation M-A requires a summary of every report,
opinion or appraisal materially related to the transaction, including the board book, which is filed as an exhibit. The
opinion section of such a proxy is longer, carries the advisor's analyses in more detail, and is written to that item. A
conventional third-party deal's DEFM14A or Schedule 14D-9 section is shorter and follows the advisor's ordinary form.

Precedents must come from the same regime. The search tags each filing: Schedule 13E-3 form, or three or more mentions of
Rule 13e-3 in a proxy, is 13e-3; the rest is conventional. Same-regime precedents rank first; the other regime is fallback.

The regime cannot be read reliably from the book: on the test set two 13e-3 deals' books read as conventional. So intake asks,
shows what the book text suggests as a hint, and records the user's answer. All 24 test deals were 13e-3 by construction
(their books are public only because Item 1015 forced them into the filing).
